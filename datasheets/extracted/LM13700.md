# LM13700 — Texas Instruments

## Description

The LM13700 consists of two current-controlled transconductance amplifiers, each with differential inputs and a push-pull output. The two amplifiers share common supplies but otherwise operate independently. Linearizing diodes are provided at the inputs to reduce distortion and allow higher input levels, resulting in a 10 dB signal-to-noise improvement referenced to 0.5% THD. High impedance buffers are provided which are especially designed to complement the dynamic range of the amplifiers. The output buffers of the LM13700 differ from those of the LM13600 in that their input bias currents (and thus their output DC levels) are independent of IABC, which may result in superior performance in audio applications.

## Features

- gm adjustable over 6 decades
- Excellent gm linearity
- Excellent matching between amplifiers
- Linearizing diodes for reduced output distortion
- High impedance buffers
- High output signal-to-noise ratio

## Typical Application Circuits

A typical voltage-controlled amplifier (VCA) uses the linearizing diodes with RD = 13k (for ID = 1 mA at +/-15V supplies), an input divider to keep differential input below 60 mVpp for less than 0.1% THD, and RBIAS = 30k for IBIAS = 1 mA. The gain is linear with the applied control voltage, providing more than 30 dB of adjustable gain range. The LM13700 is also used in voltage-controlled filters, VCOs, current-controlled impedances, and multiplexers.
